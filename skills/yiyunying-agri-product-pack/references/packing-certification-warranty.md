# Packing, Certification And Warranty

## Packing

Distinguish machine dimensions/net weight from package dimensions/gross weight.
Record package count and per-package values before aggregating totals. Calculate
theoretical CBM as `length_mm * width_mm * height_mm / 1,000,000,000` for each
package, then multiply by package count.

For CIF/CFR/CNF, confirmed CBM and destination port are central. For
DAP/DDU/DDP, confirmed gross weight and a deliverable address/postcode are
central. Formal freight requests require current confirmed packing. Estimates
may support an internal feasibility note only and must not be presented as final
freight evidence.

## Certificates and dangerous goods

Never state that CE, E-mark, COC, EPA, MSDS, UN38.3, dangerous-goods report,
battery report, brand authorization or customs paperwork exists until the exact
product/configuration and current document are verified. A similar model's
certificate is not transferable evidence.

When a carrier asks for qualification documents, report the request, document
owner, exact product, deadline and missing evidence. Do not promise or send
high-risk documents without authorization.

## Warranty, inventory and lead time

Warranty scope, spare parts, service method, inventory and production/delivery
time are product- and deployment-specific facts. Read the current approved source
before answering. Separate production time, sailing/transit time, customs and
last-mile delivery. Do not convert a general estimate into a guarantee.

## PI and shipping handoff

Before PI or shipment, lock the exact product ID, configuration, quantity,
attachments, package dimensions, gross/net weight, package count, certificates,
seller entity and trade term. Reconcile these with the quote and order; stop on
any mismatch.
